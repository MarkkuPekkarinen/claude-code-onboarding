# Inversion

Instead of asking "How do I succeed at this?", ask "How would I guarantee failure?" Map every failure path in detail, then flip each one into a concrete preventive action.

## Core Principle

Charlie Munger: *"Invert, always invert."* Most people approach goals directly. Inversion approaches them backwards. It is easier to identify what causes failure than what causes success — and eliminating failure is often more reliable than pursuing success.

## Why Inversion Works

- Success paths are often contested and uncertain. Failure paths are predictable.
- Humans are better at recognizing danger than opportunity in unfamiliar domains.
- Listing failure modes bypasses optimism bias — it is harder to be defensive about a failure you invented than one you are living.
- The flip from failure to action produces more specific, concrete preventive measures than forward-planning alone.

## Interactive Method

### Step 1: State the Goal

Confirm the user's goal in one sentence. It must be specific enough to fail in specific ways. "Be successful" is too vague. "Launch a B2B SaaS product with 10 paying customers within 6 months" is specific enough.

If the goal is vague, ask: *"What does success look like specifically — by when, and measured how?"*

### Step 2: Generate Failure Modes

Ask: *"Imagine you committed fully to this goal and it failed completely. What went wrong?"*

Then push for more:
- *"What else could cause failure?"*
- *"What failure would be most embarrassing?"*
- *"What failure would be hardest to recover from?"*
- *"What failure is most likely even if you do everything right?"*

**Failure categories to ensure coverage:**

| Category | Probing Question |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Execution** | Where does the work itself break down? |
| **People** | Who could fail, leave, or resist? |
| **Assumptions** | What belief, if wrong, destroys the plan? |
| **External** | What happens if the market, competitor, or environment shifts? |
| **Timing** | What if this takes 3x longer or costs 3x more? |
| **Own behavior** | What decision could you make that would guarantee failure? |

### Step 3: Force Specificity

Generic failure modes are useless. Push until each one is specific.

| Vague | Specific |
|-------|----------|
| "Poor execution" | "We ship 3 months late because two engineers left and we didn't hire replacements in time" |
| "Market won't respond" | "Conversion from free trial to paid is under 2% because the product solves a problem users don't experience as painful" |
| "We run out of money" | "We burn through runway in month 4 because CAC is 4x what we modeled and we don't cut spending fast enough" |
| "Team falls apart" | "The founding team disagrees on whether to stay B2B or go B2C at month 5 when initial B2B results are mixed" |

For each failure, ask: *"When would this happen? What would trigger it? Who would be responsible for catching it?"*

### Step 4: Rank Failure Modes

Rank each failure mode on two axes:

| Axis | Options |
|------|---------|
| **Likelihood** | High / Medium / Low |
| **Reversibility** | Recoverable / Costly to recover / Fatal |

Prioritize: High likelihood + hard to recover = address first.

### Step 5: Flip Each to a Preventive Action

For each failure mode, ask: *"What is the one concrete action that would prevent or significantly reduce this failure?"*

The action must be:
- Specific (not "be more careful")
- Actionable now or within a defined timeframe
- Assigned to someone (not "the team")
- Measurable (how will you know the action worked?)

### Step 6: Check for Blind Spots

After generating failure modes, run the inversion check:

*"What would you do if your goal was to guarantee failure as fast as possible?"*

This surfaces failure modes the user hasn't listed — often the most important ones, because they involve the user's own behavior rather than external factors.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | What to Do Instead |
|---------|-------------------|
| Generic failure modes that apply to any project | Push for specific triggers, timings, and mechanisms |
| Only listing external failures | Inversion is most powerful on own-behavior failures — push there |
| Preventive actions that are actually just goals ("communicate better") | Actions must describe a specific behavior change, not an outcome |
| Stopping at 3 failure modes | Keep going until you hit at least 5; the interesting ones are often later |
| Treating inversion as pessimism | Frame it as a systematic way to increase success probability, not catastrophizing |
